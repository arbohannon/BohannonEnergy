Understanding BER Ratings

The Building Energy Rating (BER) allows prospective buyers or tenants to objectively compare the energy performance of different dwellings on a like-for-like basis. It helps you to understand the energy efficiency of a home and can be used as an indicator of the running costs and carbon emissions associated with heating the home to a comfortable level. The rating scale looks similar to the energy rating labels for household appliances. It rates the home on a scale from A0-G. A0 is a new category for the most energy‑efficient buildings that do not use fossil fuels. A-rated homes are the most energy-efficient and comfortable. They tend to have the lowest energy bills. G-rated homes are the least energy-efficient. They typically require a lot of energy to heat the home and have the highest energy bills.

For more detailed information on how BER is calculated and what affects your rating, visit the SEAI Understand a BER Rating page.

Why Do You Need a BER Cert?

Common reasons why you would need to get a BER assessment for your property:

SEAI Grant

The SEAI currently offer very attractive grants for home energy upgrades. To avail of these grants you will need an updated BER cert upon completion of the upgrade works.

Selling Property

If you are planning on selling your property you will need to have a valid BER cert.

Letting Property

If you are planning on renting (letting) your property you will need to hold a valid BER rating.

Green Mortgage

If you are applying for a green mortgage, your bank may request an up-to-date BER for your property.

New Build

All new build homes must have a final BER assessment completed (and a BER rating issued) before being offered for sale/let or being occupied for the first time.

Other

If you happen to need a BER assessment completed for any other reason please get in touch with us.

Ireland's New BER Scale (Effective May 2026)

The Building Energy Rating (BER) system in Ireland was updated on 24 May 2026 to align with EU standards and simplify the rating scale.

Simplified Rating Scale

The previous 15-level scale (A1, A2, B1, B2, B3, etc.) has been replaced by a streamlined set of eight categories: A0 | A | B | C | D | E | F | G. This aligns Ireland's energy performance standards with other EU member states for direct cross-border comparability.

The A0 Category: Zero-Fossil-Fuel Homes

The new A0 rating recognises buildings achieving exceptional energy performance while operating entirely without fossil fuels. To qualify for A0, a dwelling must meet:

  • Primary energy value of 42 kWh/m² per year or below
  • Carbon equivalent emissions of 5 kg CO₂eq/m² per year or below
  • No fossil fuels on site
  • Energy use met entirely by carbon-free sources or in compliance with national criteria
  • Capacity to respond to external signals and adjust energy consumption

Existing Certificates and Grant Eligibility

All existing BER certificates remain valid for ten years from their date of issue and continue to satisfy mandatory requirements for sale or rental. SEAI grant eligibility under the One Stop Shop scheme is unchanged in substance: the requirement to achieve a post-works rating of B2 is now expressed as achieving a B rating (which encompasses the equivalent energy performance threshold).

Enhanced Information on New Certificates

BER certificates issued under the new system display the property's estimated energy consumption and the proportion of energy derived from renewable sources. Each certificate includes a QR code linking directly to the property's BER assessment record and tailored upgrade recommendations.

This reform supports Ireland's legally binding target to retrofit 500,000 homes to a B rating by 2030 by making performance data more legible, comparable, and connected to financial products and planning decisions.
